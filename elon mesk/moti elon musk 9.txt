First Principles Thinking
“It’s important to reason from first principles rather than by analogy.”
“The normal way people think is by analogy—copying what others do.”
“That’s mentally easier, but it limits innovation.”
What First Principles Means
“Boil things down to the most fundamental truths.”
“Ask: what do we know is definitely true?”
“Then reason up from there.”
Difficulty of Deep Thinking
“First principles thinking takes more mental energy.”
“That’s why most people don’t do it.”
Challenging Assumptions
“Just because something has always been expensive doesn’t mean it always will be.”
“If you assume the future equals the past, you can’t create anything new.”
Example: Batteries
“People say batteries are expensive because they’ve always been expensive.”
“That’s flawed thinking.”
Breaking Problems Down
“Ask: what are the raw materials in a battery?”
“Look at the actual cost of those materials.”
“The raw materials are much cheaper than people think.”
Insight from First Principles
“If materials are cheap, the problem is just how to combine them efficiently.”
“That means batteries can be much cheaper than assumed.”
Innovation Philosophy
“Don’t accept conventional wisdom without questioning it.”
“Rebuild solutions from the ground up.”
Core Theme
True innovation comes from rethinking problems from fundamentals, not copying existing models.
