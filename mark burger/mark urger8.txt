1. Start simple & build fast
“I started writing the site and launched it at Harvard in February 2004.”
“Within a couple of weeks, a few thousand people had signed up.”
“We started getting emails from other colleges asking us to launch it.”

👉 Key idea: Launch early → let demand guide expansion.

2. Learn fast, don’t wait to be ready
“If you have a good background in C, you can pick up PHP in a day or two.”
“The internet is a pretty good tool… that’s how we learned most of it.”

👉 Key idea: Speed of learning matters more than prior expertise.

3. Work with smart people
“The most important thing is to have smart people.”
“We don’t hire people for specific skills—we hire for intelligence.”
“The most performant people are younger people who learn quickly.”

👉 Key idea: Raw intelligence + adaptability > fixed skillsets.

4. Focus on building, not over-planning
“We were making stuff instead of talking to lawyers early on.”
“It’s more useful to make things happen and apologize later than to over-plan and not get stuff done.”

👉 Key idea: Action beats perfection and bureaucracy.

5. Core idea behind Facebook
“I just wanted to make something where people can type in someone’s name and get information about them.”
“Wanting to look people up is a core human desire.”

👉 Key idea: Simple, fundamental human behavior drives great products.

6. Scaling through smart architecture
“The computation of connections grows exponentially—n squared, n cubed.”
“We split databases by school to make it manageable.”
“Instead of scaling over millions, we scaled over smaller groups.”

👉 Key idea: Good architecture solves scale before it becomes a problem.

7. Iterate instead of perfecting
“It doesn’t have to be perfect the first time.”
“As long as the architecture is right, you can fix implementation later.”

👉 Key idea: Build → test → fix → repeat.

8. Technology leverage is unprecedented
“You can rent machines for $100 a month and scale to hundreds of thousands of users.”
“Individuals are able to do way more than ever before.”

👉 Key idea: Small teams can now build massive systems.

9. Don’t fear big competitors
“Instead of worrying about Google, you can just get a lot of stuff done.”

👉 Key idea: Execution matters more than competition.

10. Platform & growth thinking
“We want to maximize long-term value, not short-term money.”
“We’re always building infrastructure.”

👉 Key idea: Think long-term; keep investing in the foundation.

11. Privacy philosophy
“Information should be available to the people you want it to be available to.”
“We give people complete control over what they share.”

👉 Key idea: User control builds trust and sustainability.

12. Social solutions > technical solutions
“People use real names, so they behave better.”
“That solves problems that would otherwise need complex systems.”

👉 Key idea: Design behavior, not just systems.

13. Decision-making on technology
“Trust the smart people.”
“Build consensus among engineers.”

👉 Key idea: Good teams outperform rigid processes.

14. Product development mindset
“We anticipate problems, evaluate options, and make decent decisions.”
“It wasn’t upfront genius—just solving problems as they came.”

👉 Key idea: Success = continuous problem-solving, not perfect foresight.

15. Meritocracy in teams
“People who build the best things get the most influence.”
“We try to be as much of a meritocracy as possible.”

👉 Key idea: Reward execution and impact.

16. Learning philosophy
“Take the hardest courses—you learn the most when challenged.”
“Framework matters more than perfect optimization.”

👉 Key idea: Deep thinking > surface efficiency.

17. On company origins
“Most successful companies start from someone trying to make something cool—not trying to build a company.”

👉 Key idea: Great companies are byproducts of great ideas.

18. On innovation & ideas
“We have a unique platform—others can’t easily replicate what we build.”
“A lot of ideas come from people just experimenting.”

👉 Key idea: Innovation emerges from freedom and context.

19. On simplicity in product design
“Facebook is a directory—everyone’s page should be structured the same way.”
“Consistency makes it easier to use.”

👉 Key idea: Simplicity and standardization improve usability.

20. Core philosophy of execution
“At each point, we picked out problems quickly and made decisions.”
“We had enough competence to evaluate options and execute.”

👉 Key idea: Speed + judgment > perfect planning.

🧠 One-Line Summary

Build simple things fast, learn quickly, work with smart people, and scale through good architecture—while focusing on real human behavior and long-term value.
