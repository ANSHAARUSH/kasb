1. Bet on Visionary People
Great breakthroughs start with believing in bold ideas and the people behind them.
Pixar began by backing a long-term creative vision.

Key quote:

“I bought into that dream both financially and spiritually.”
2. Long-Term Commitment Pays Off
Meaningful innovation takes time—often far longer than expected.

Key quote:

“It took us ten years to do that.”
3. Create the Right Environment (Not Do Everything Yourself)
Leaders shouldn’t try to do the work—they should enable great people to do it.

Key quotes:

“What I try to do is help create the environment where… people can make films.”
“My greatest joy is when we have people that are much better than I am.”
4. Combine Art + Technology
Breakthroughs happen at the intersection of creative and technical excellence.
Pixar succeeded by blending Hollywood storytelling with Silicon Valley engineering.

Key insight:

The rare advantage is attracting the best from both worlds and making them collaborate.
5. Build Teams of A-Players Only
Talent density is everything.
Great people want to work with other great people.

Key quotes:

“The very best people will only go to work in a few places.”
“We were able to hire 125 A-plus people.”
6. Management’s Job = Support Talent
The role of leadership is to serve the team, not control it.
Power flows to those doing the real work.

Key quotes:

“The hierarchy of power inverts… the CEO is actually at the bottom.”
“I sort of feel like I work for most of these people.”
7. Remove Obstacles, Don’t Micromanage
Build great teams, then clear their path.

Key quote:

“Creating the right environment… and removing obstacles out of their way.”
8. Culture > Contracts
Great culture retains people better than forced agreements.
People should stay because they want to, not because they’re locked in.

Key quotes:

“We don’t take anybody for granted.”
“Every single day, we worry about how to make Pixar a better company.”
9. Ownership > Work-for-Hire
Avoid businesses where you don’t own what you create.
Long-term value comes from ownership.

Key quotes:

“You don’t share in any of the upside of what you create.”
“We own a piece of what we create.”
10. Focus on What Matters Most
Don’t spread talent across low-impact work.
Allocate your best people to high-value opportunities.

Key quote:

“We couldn’t afford to have 25 of them making commercials anymore.”
11. Learn from the Best
Partnering with great organizations accelerates learning.

Key quote:

“We learned a tremendous amount… you can’t buy that experience.”
12. Plan Before You Build
In complex work (like animation), you must design everything upfront.

Key quote:

“You have to edit your film before you make it.”
13. Technology Evolves, Stories Endure
Tech products become obsolete quickly.
Great stories last generations.

Key quotes:

“All these technology boxes… sooner or later become part of the sedimentary layer.”
“These stories renew themselves with each generation.”
14. Story Is Everything
No amount of technology can fix a weak story.

Key quote:

“No amount of technology can turn a bad story into a good story.”
“It’s the story, stupid.”
15. Aim for Timeless Impact
Create work that lasts decades, not just years.

Key quote:

“People are going to be watching Toy Story in 60 years… because of the story.”
16. Ambition Scales with Technology
As tools improve, expectations and creative ambition rise equally.

Key quote:

“Our ambitions… are growing as fast as the technology can feed them.”
17. Rare Opportunities Matter
Building something culturally meaningful is a privilege.

Key quote:

“It’s a rare opportunity… to put these stories into the culture.”
Final Core Message
Back great people and bold visions.
Build environments where talent thrives.
Focus on ownership, quality, and long-term impact.
And above all: great stories outlast great technology.
